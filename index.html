A practical guide to studying your competitors' ads

If you run paid ads, your competitors are publishing their strategy in public every day. Every ad they run on Facebook, Instagram, Google and YouTube is visible to anyone who knows where to look, and the ads that keep running are usually the ones that make them money. This guide explains how to turn that public information into decisions for your own campaigns.

The one signal that matters most: how long an ad has run

Brands switch off ads that lose money. So when an ad has been running for weeks or months, that's a strong hint it's working. Longevity is a better signal than likes or comments, which can be bought or skewed by a single viral moment.

A real example: on 8 November we looked at AG1, the greens-powder brand. They had roughly 1,300 active Meta ads in the US, plus a Google video ad that had been running for 146 days. You don't keep paying for a video ad for nearly five months unless it earns its keep. That ad is worth studying closely: the hook, the offer, the length, the call to action.

Where to look by hand

  • Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) shows active ads across Facebook and Instagram. Search the brand, set the country, and look at the start dates.
  • Google Ads Transparency Center shows a brand's search, display and YouTube ads.

Both are free. The downside is time. Scrolling through hundreds of ads, noting start dates and grouping similar creative can take an afternoon for a single competitor.

What to write down

For each competitor, build a short table:

  1. Ad count. How many ads are live right now? A sudden jump means a launch or a test.
  2. Start date. Which ads are oldest? Those are the proven ones.
  3. Format. Video, static image, carousel?
  4. Hook. The first line or first three seconds.
  5. Offer. Discount, free trial, bundle, guarantee?
  6. Landing page. Where does the click go?

Patterns appear quickly. When we checked Gymshark, about 380 US Meta ads were live and almost all had started that same week, which tells you they were in the middle of a fresh push rather than coasting on old winners. That's a different story from AG1's long-running evergreen ads, and it calls for a different response.

Turning research into action

  • Borrow structure, not creative. If long-running competitor ads open with a customer problem in the first two seconds, test that structure with your own message. Don't copy their words or footage.
  • Find the gaps. If nobody in your market runs ads about price, guarantees or speed, that may be your angle.
  • Time your pushes. When a competitor launches a big batch, expect auction prices to move.
  • Re-check monthly. One snapshot is interesting; a monthly series shows you strategy.
